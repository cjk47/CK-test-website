XVIVO Scientific Animation is a medical animation studio producing mechanism of action videos, surgical training content, and patient education materials for pharmaceutical and biotech companies worldwide.

The Challenge

XVIVO creates scientific visuals for pharmaceutical and biotech companies, including mechanism of action videos, clinical trial animations, and patient education content. They came to me after a drop in traffic and rankings for key animation-related searches.

A few things were holding the site back:

  • Service pages didn't reflect how buyers actually search or evaluate vendors
  • Most content targeted broad terms instead of specific animation types
  • The portfolio generated multiple versions of the same content through URL parameters
  • Search engines were spending time crawling low-value pages while important ones were harder to find and index
  • Blog content did not link to service pages, making it difficult for users to move from research to action

In a niche B2B market where a single client relationship is worth hundreds of thousands of dollars, losing ground in search wasn't a visibility problem — it was a revenue problem.

The Strategy

  • Fix the Technical Foundation First

    Before touching a word of content, I crawled the entire site to find what was quietly working against it — redirect chains, indexing gaps, pages draining authority to dead ends. The audit surfaced 30+ technical issues, and I worked through every one of them before moving on. A content strategy built on a broken foundation doesn't hold.

  • Rewrite Metadata to Match How Customers Search

    Title tags and meta descriptions across 40+ pages were rewritten to match how customers search. A pharmaceutical team looking to hire an animation studio isn't just searching for a "medical animation company" — they're searching for the specific type of animation they need, whether it's mechanism of action or oncology patient education. Each page needed metadata that spoke to those searches directly, not a one-size-fits-all description of the company.

  • Build Topic Depth Around Medical Animation

    I built a topic cluster architecture around medical animation, creating dedicated service pages for every major animation type: mechanism of action, neurology, oncology, clinical trial recruitment, patient education, and more. Each page targets its own set of long-tail queries while reinforcing XVIVO's topical authority across the entire medical animation category.

  • Turn Portfolio Filters Into Indexable Pages

    XVIVO's portfolio filtered by animation type, but those filtered views relied on URL parameters. Although Google can index parameterized URLs, without unique content it usually treats them as duplicates and skips them — and that's exactly what was happening here.

    Converting each filter into its own standalone page fixed that. Clean descriptive URL, unique copy explaining the animation type and typical use cases, titles and metadata aligned to the target search. Pages that were previously getting ignored became direct entry points for searches like "neurology medical animation" or "clinical trial recruitment animation."

  • Match Each Page to What Searchers Actually Want

    I audited each key page and made changes based on what people searching for that term actually want to see. On the homepage, someone searching for a medical animation company wants to see the work, not read about it. Moving real video examples above the fold reduced bounce rates and gave search engines a stronger signal that the page satisfied the intent of the searcher.

  • Funnel Blog Traffic to Service Pages

    Blog content was pulling in readers with no clear path forward. I restructured internal links so that posts connected directly to relevant service pages, making it easy for someone doing research to take a next step.

The Results

Within three months of implementation, XVIVO's organic performance showed clear, measurable improvement across every tracked metric.

The content and technical fixes compounded quickly — more pages indexed correctly meant more impressions, better metadata meant better click-through rates, and better landing pages meant more inquiries.
