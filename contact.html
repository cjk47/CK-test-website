SEO timelines depend on competition, domain age, indexing, and execution. Some wins appear in 3 months, but realistically you should plan for 6–12 months with quarterly reviews. SEO is a long-term channel — it builds stable, compounding growth rather than quick spikes. That said, site improvements made early on will benefit user experience and conversion rates even before rankings move.

Content strategy covers all stages of the buyer's journey — transactional, commercial, and informational. For newer sites, I work backwards: nail the conversion-focused pages first, then build out topic coverage from there.

  • Transactional: product/service landing pages
  • Commercial: comparisons, reviews, alternatives
  • Informational: blog posts that answer questions and position your solution

SEO is more cost-effective long-term — once you rank, you earn consistent traffic without paying per click. It's especially powerful for local search and lets you reach users at every stage of the funnel through different content types.

That said, SEO and paid search work best together. When a brand appears in both organic and paid results for the same keyword, it reinforces credibility and increases the likelihood of getting the click. Shared keywords and PPC copy insights can inform and strengthen your organic strategy.

Organic search drives more traffic than any other channel — on average 53% of all site traffic, compared to 15% from paid and 5% from social. Organic listings also cover more real estate in search results and are far more trusted: 94% of clicks go to organic results vs. 6% to paid ads.

Beyond traffic, SEO improves site accessibility and user experience, and its effects compound over time — making it one of the highest-ROI investments a business can make in its digital presence.
